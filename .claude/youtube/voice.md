# ViralNovaLabs channel voice profile

## Channel and audience
An English-language documentary channel covering a wide range of subjects. Address an intelligent general audience: curious viewers who want clear explanations, meaningful context, and a satisfying conclusion without needing specialist knowledge.

## Narration language and delivery
- Narration: English (US-neutral, internationally understandable).
- Subtitles: Persian, produced by the repository subtitle pipeline. Do not switch the spoken narration to Persian unless a specific video brief requests it.
- Delivery: confident, calm, cinematic documentary narration. Sound informed and human, not like an advertisement or an overexcited trailer.
- Explain technical terms in plain English on first use. Vary sentence length; use short sentences for emphasis and longer ones for context.
- Adapt the degree of drama to the subject. Do not force mystery, danger, or sensationalism onto ordinary topics.

## Editorial approach
- Open with a concrete question, surprising verified fact, vivid scene, or consequential moment. Do not use generic greetings or a long channel introduction.
- Build a clear narrative: question/context, evidence and explanation, complication or counterpoint, then a meaningful payoff.
- Separate verified facts from hypotheses, disputed claims, estimates, and speculation.
- Prefer primary sources, credible research, and named sources. Never invent citations, quotations, statistics, dates, expert opinions, or footage provenance.
- When a claim cannot be verified, qualify it or omit it.
- Do not repeat the same point to inflate runtime. Every section must add evidence, context, or narrative movement.

## Words and habits to avoid
Avoid empty hype and generic AI phrasing such as: "game-changer", "mind-blowing", "unlock", "dive into", "let's get into it", "in today's video", "you won't believe", and "the truth will shock you".
Avoid fake cliffhangers, unsupported superlatives, repetitive rhetorical questions, and calls to action that interrupt the story.

## Claims and trust
- Never claim certainty where evidence is mixed.
- Never fabricate channel results, view counts, audience reactions, credentials, or personal experience.
- Do not present generated reconstructions as authentic archival footage.
- Flag sensitive, graphic, or potentially misleading visual material for editorial review.
- Respect copyright and licensing; record the source/license for stock and archival assets.

## Formats and runtime
- Shorts: normally 45–60 seconds for the existing daily production workflow; one clear idea and one payoff.
- Long-form documentaries: support 15, 30, 45, 60 minutes or longer when requested. Use a detailed outline, chapter structure, source notes, and a sustained narrative arc. Never pad a script merely to hit a runtime.
- Long-form transcripts should produce validated YouTube chapters and may be repurposed into Shorts with fresh hooks.

## Visual and subtitle notes
- Write literal, topic-specific visual instructions for each narration beat. Avoid generic or unrelated stock footage.
- Keep Persian subtitles concise, accurately translated, synchronized, and visually unobtrusive. The production pipeline is responsible for rendering them.
- Do not put essential on-screen text beneath subtitle overlays.
